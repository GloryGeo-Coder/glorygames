## Model assumptions and teaching limits

- The low-voltage circuit compares specified copper, graphite and rubber specimens. Graphite is a conducting form of non-metal carbon. The activity does not imply that every non-metal insulates or that every carbon form conducts. It provides no mains-electricity instructions.
- Handle comparisons use invented far-end warm-up markers under fixed conditions. They illustrate relative heat transfer, not real product safety ratings or a complete materials specification.
- The pure-water trace assumes fixed normal atmospheric pressure and continuing heat input. Its boiling plateau is near 100 °C; melting is separately identified near 0 °C. The records are schematic and do not claim that every liquid boils at the same temperature or that local pressure never matters.
- Magnetic separation accounts for six iron, six aluminium and six plastic markers. Equal piece sizes mean the specified size sieve cannot distinguish their material types. Aluminium is still a metal despite its different magnetic response.
- The virtual distillation models only water and non-volatile salt. Cooling vapour into a receiver distinguishes collection from evaporation alone. Six salt and twelve water markers remain accounted for. No construction, heating, solvent-handling or drinking-water instructions are supplied.
- Paper chromatography uses water-soluble model inks and water. Four pigment markers produce one or two observed bands, with a water-only blank. A single detected band does not prove purity or uniquely identify a substance. Band positions are illustrative rather than measured retention factors.
- The litmus examples use a specified acidic vinegar model, a specified basic soap model and an idealised neutral pure-water control. Real formulations and water samples can differ. Red paper staying red alone cannot distinguish acid from neutral; blue staying blue alone cannot distinguish base from neutral. The game uses both readings and never asks learners to taste, touch or mix unknown substances.
- The four-component recovery plan accounts for six iron, six sand, six salt and twelve water markers. Filtering alone leaves material types mixed. Magnetic separation, filtration and evaporation can recover solids while water remains uncollected in the air. Condensation enables a separate water collection in the selected distillation plan; no material is described as destroyed or certified for drinking.
- The exhibition ledger uses invented new-resource tokens for the same twenty-delivery brief. An existing sound case and a new case both protect the exhibit; reuse requires fewer tokens in that ledger. A film envelope fails the protection requirement. These data are not carbon measurements, a real life-cycle assessment or a universal metal/plastic ranking.
- The first-twenty guide supplies all twenty names and symbols, coordinates and categories. Its card list follows atomic-number order; the SVG overview preserves the standard group columns, including the empty group 3–12 region for these elements. Boron and silicon are common school semi-metal examples. Hydrogen is identified as a non-metal despite its left-hand position, and properties are not treated as identical within every category.

## Learning and progression

Fifteen investigations use the existing predict–compare–explain–apply sequence, progressive hints and untimed retries. A correct explanation and two distinct recorded settings are required before applying a discovery. Some briefs have multiple working choices. The element guide is a reference aid; selecting cards does not award points or substitute for the investigation evidence.

Grade 7 unlocks after the fifteen Grade 4–6 missions. Earlier lessons, stories and save data remain compatible. Only the three new element-guide activities add an optional validated reference-symbol field to their laboratory records; older lab record shapes remain unchanged under save schema version 1. Completing Grades 4–7 gives sixty saved discoveries. Grades 8–10 and full assessment/revision coverage remain later work, with unavailable grades not shown as playable.
