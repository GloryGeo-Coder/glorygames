## Scientific interpretation and model limits

- CO, CO₂ and SO₃ use mono/di/tri oxygen-count prefixes. CO and CO₂ are distinct compounds; SO₂ and SO₃ are also distinct. Exact element-symbol case matters. The first-twenty reference guide supports the three ledger investigations and their replays. Copper (Cu), outside that guide, is explicitly named in its own equation activity.
- NaCl represents a 1:1 ratio in an extended ionic solid, not separate NaCl molecules. The correct sixteen-ion slice contains eight Na⁺ and eight Cl⁻ with balanced charge. The other proposals deliberately violate that specified ratio and neutrality. The illustration is a 2D slice rather than complete 3D geometry.
- Equation coefficients change quantities; changing product subscripts changes the specified substance. Valid records 2 Mg + O₂ → 2 MgO and 2 Cu + O₂ → 2 CuO retain every atom. MgO and CuO are counted as formula units of ionic solids rather than isolated molecules. The changed-formula and incomplete-count records are proposals to reject, not actual reaction outcomes. This increment provides guided equation selection and inventory interpretation, not unrestricted equation writing or complete assessment coverage.
- MgO and the specified copper(II) oxide CuO are metal oxides and can act as bases in these examples; CO₂ is a non-metal oxide. This does not imply that all metal oxides behave identically or that every oxygen-containing compound belongs to the same family.
- Twelve iron sites remain accounted for. Bare, continuous-paint and scratched-paint records expose twelve, zero and three sites, respectively, during the same moist-air interval. Rusting requires oxygen and moisture; actual rust is a complex mixture including hydrated iron oxides. Markers are qualitative, and the intact-barrier assumption does not promise indefinite real coating life.
- The specified carbon-dioxide record preserves one C and two O atoms. Other combustion conditions can produce CO or other products; a CO-only proposal is not treated as this complete CO₂ record with an unexplained missing oxygen atom. The sulfur-dioxide record preserves one S and two O atoms; SO₃ would require a different inventory. No combustion procedure is supplied.
- Rain examples distinguish mild background acidity associated with dissolved CO₂ from additional acid deposition associated with SO₂ and nitrogen oxides. The [US EPA explanation](https://www.epa.gov/acidrain/what-acid-rain?format=Articles) supports that distinction. Example readings 5.6 and 4.2 are illustrative, not fixed values for all rain or a unique pollutant-identification test. Atmospheric chemistry is more complex than a single water/oxide step.
- Aqueous pH classification uses approximately 25 °C: below 7 acidic, 7 neutral, above 7 basic. The displayed 0–14 school scale is not a universal bound. pH is logarithmic, not a linear amount to average. Intrinsic acid/base strength and solution concentration are distinct; one pH reading does not identify both.
- Blue litmus turning red supports acidity but not the narrow pH 2–4 brief. A supplied universal-indicator chart estimates that range; a specified calibrated meter reads 3.0. Both can meet the brief, with different precision. Indicator charts and calibration matter. A reading is not a sample identity or drinking-water certificate.
- The virtual neutralisation tracks six acid-equivalent counters and three/six/nine base-equivalent counters. HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O accounts for salt, newly formed water and excess reactants. Given pH records 2/7/12 are illustrative sensor observations, not a formula inferred from counter numbers, a mixing recipe or an average of initial pH. The matched dilute strong-acid/strong-base example near 25 °C is neutral; other reactants and temperatures need not give pH 7 at equivalence. [RSC Education](https://edu.rsc.org/cpd/acids-and-bases/2000001.article) discusses neutralisation and temperature dependence; [OpenStax](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry/pages/14-4-hydrolysis-of-salt-solutions) distinguishes strong/weak acid/base salt behaviour.
- The HCl/MgO activity forms magnesium chloride and water, preserving H, Cl, Mg and O. A metal oxide differs from the metal itself; the metal/acid hydrogen rule must not be substituted here. The wrong-salt and hydrogen-product proposals fail the specified record. Salt does not always mean sodium chloride.
- Soil records 4.5, 6.5 and 7.9 are supplied illustrative outcomes against a greenhouse-specific target of 6–7. Limestone can reduce acidity, but no treatment quantities, linear dose-response or universal crop target are asserted. Real soil buffering, time and crop requirements matter; matching pH does not clear contamination.
- The planning ledger starts at twelve SO₂ and eight nitrogen-oxide emission markers. Source controls leave three and two, a 75% reduction in each, using the six-token budget. Local acidity treatment may address effects but leaves those continuing source emissions unchanged in this ledger. Invented tokens are not measured carbon footprints, actual technology performance or a complete environmental assessment.

## Progression and continuity

Every investigation preserves predict–compare–explain–apply, distinct recorded settings, progressive hints and untimed retries. Multiple working choices are accepted for metal-oxide identification and evidence precision. Oversupplying base or exceeding the soil target fails the stated brief.

Grade 9 opens after the twenty-five Grade 4–8 missions. Completing it gives thirty adventure missions and ninety saved investigation records. Earlier lesson/story/audio definitions, save schema version 1 and the original twenty-mission dash are retained. Grade 10 and final game-information-page editorial updates remain later increments.
