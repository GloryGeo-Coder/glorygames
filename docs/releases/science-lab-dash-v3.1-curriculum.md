This increment covers selected Grade 5 Matter and Materials / Processing outcomes. It is not a complete Grade 5 Natural Sciences course or a formal assessment instrument.

The stories, prompts, explanations and diagrams are original teaching content. Sources guide topic selection; textbook or assessment questions are not reproduced.

## Model assumptions

- Metal examples are specific specimens. Copper is used for malleability, ductility and heat conduction; iron is strongly attracted by the classroom magnet. Copper and aluminium are still metals when they are not strongly attracted. Appearance, magnetism and heat conduction are different properties.
- Rust means iron rust. Water and oxygen must both be available in the idealised time-lapse. Dry-air and oxygen-excluded comparisons assume the named component is absent. An intact paint barrier protects the model surface; a chip exposes iron. Other metals can corrode without forming iron rust.
- Process comparisons show stages of the same virtual starting materials. Setting, curing, cooking and cooling are distinguished; elapsed time is compressed. Concrete hardening is not described as evaporation of all its water. Set jelly is a gel containing water.
- The water vessel is specifically a fired ceramic with an intact fired glaze. Dry clay and all unglazed ceramics are not described as universally waterproof.
- Weaving demonstrates one over-and-under reed pattern. It acknowledges other patterns, coiling and plaiting rather than presenting one method as universal across African communities.
- Both clear glass and clear rigid acrylic satisfy the stated viewing brief. Both lined containers satisfy the dry indoor delivery brief. These comparisons deliberately allow multiple working designs.
- Diagrams are schematic and not to scale. No real temperatures, mixing ratios, firing instructions, structural load ratings or packaging safety certifications are supplied. Learning does not require real laboratory materials or tasting samples.

## Learning sequence

Each activity accepts any prediction, requires observations from at least two different settings and then asks the learner to explain the evidence. Incorrect explanations can be retried; two progressive hints are available. The successful setting must be the last tested setting before a discovery can be applied. Thirty permanent notebook entries span the two grades, with no extra points for replaying an already recorded discovery.

Grade 4’s original fifteen investigations remain unchanged. Grade 5 unlocks after the five Grade 4 missions, followed by a continuous five-mission path. Grades 6–10 remain future increments and are not shown as playable content.
