## Model assumptions and scientific limits

- Eighteen schematic dots represent each particle arrangement. Solids vibrate around fixed positions, liquids have close particles that can rearrange, and gases have much wider spacing. Dot size and spacing are not quantitative measurements. Reduced-motion settings stop the diagram animations.
- The sieve accounts separately for six model beans and eighteen sand markers above and below the screen. Filters account for six solid markers in the residue or filtrate. Separation does not destroy material.
- The salt and sugar doses are below saturation during soluble/insoluble comparisons. Both can form solutions. Dissolving is not described as melting, and a solution remains a mixture even when solute grains cannot be seen.
- Ordinary filter paper retains the specified sand grains, while dissolved salt and sugar pass through. This does not claim that every filter has identical capabilities or that the collected water is potable.
- Evaporation diagrams conserve six salt and twelve water markers in each before/after view. The final recovery snapshot represents enough elapsed time for water to move into the air as vapour. No boiling is required by the activity, and the salt-recovery task does not collect drinking water.
- Rate bars use invented comparison units, not measured seconds. All trials start with the same soluble dose and reach the same endpoint. Each bench changes one named factor and keeps the others fixed. Faster dissolving is distinguished from a higher saturation limit.
- The saturation model holds temperature and water amount fixed and has an illustrative capacity of four units. Four units produce a saturated solution without residue. Six produce the same saturated solution plus two excess units. The activity’s target is visible evidence of excess, so the four-unit option is correctly described as saturated while not meeting that particular goal. It does not explain saturation as filling literal gaps between water particles.
- Wetland peak-flow markers are an illustrative comparison, not a water-volume balance or a hydraulic forecast. The protected case represents habitat, flow regulation and some sediment retention; it does not promise removal of every pollutant or safe drinking water.
- Both filtered treatment routes remove the twelve specified visible markers. The route with settling has a lower final-filter load: screening retains four, settling retains six and filtering retains two. Dissolved and microbial hazards remain separate and unassessed by this visible-solids model.
- Managed disinfection can address germs under verified conditions while chemical hazards remain unresolved. No success state certifies drinking-water safety. The final sample is held for qualified assessment, and the game instructs learners not to drink experiment water.

## Learning and progression

Fifteen new investigations retain the predict–compare–explain–apply sequence. Learners record at least two different settings, test a setting that meets the stated goal and then explain their evidence. Predictions and explanations can be revised without life penalties; progressive hints remain available.

Grade 6 unlocks after the ten Grade 4–5 missions. Existing checkpoints, choice orders, trial history, scores and notebook entries remain compatible with save schema version 1. Completing all three paths yields forty-five permanent discoveries; replaying an already recorded activity does not farm score. Grades 7–10 remain future increments and are not presented as playable content.
