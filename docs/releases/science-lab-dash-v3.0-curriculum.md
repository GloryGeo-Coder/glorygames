# Grade 4 curriculum mapping

This chapter supports selected Grade 4 Natural Sciences and Technology content in Term 2, Matter and Materials and Structures. It is not a complete Grade 4 science course. Future grade paths are roadmap items and are not advertised as playable content.

Primary reference: Department of Basic Education, [CAPS Natural Sciences and Technology Grades 4–6](https://www.education.gov.za/LinkClick.aspx?fileticket=IzbFrpzoQ44%3D), printed pages 20–22 (PDF pages 24–26). These pages cover states and changes of state, raw/manufactured materials, material properties, hollow paper pillars, rolled struts and triangular frames.

| Mission | Selected curriculum content | Playable evidence |
|---|---|---|
| The mixed-up storeroom | Properties of solids, liquids and gases | Compare shape, liquid transfer and containment of air |
| The cold-room trail | Melting, condensation and freezing | Compare the final states of the same water sample |
| The materials workshop | Raw/manufactured materials; waterproofing and flexibility | Test labelled specimens and select a suitable material |
| The bridge of ideas | Strengthening paper; hollow pillars and stable frames | Compare paper forms, roof supports and diagonal bracing |
| The Element Archive | Applying material properties; evaporation; manufacturing origins | Protect a crate, investigate drying without boiling and repair a material record |

## Teaching-model boundaries

Water-state comparisons assume normal atmospheric pressure and sufficient time to reach the displayed final state. The chosen temperatures avoid phase boundaries. Evaporation is taught separately: water can evaporate without boiling, and a cover can trap vapour without eliminating evaporation. Water vapour is invisible; dots are representations, not visible vapour or a scale drawing. Particle counts stay consistent across the model views.

Waterproofing outcomes describe the named untreated specimens in a fixed illustrative 100 mL test. They are not universal claims about every paper, fabric or plastic. Structure outcomes indicate whether the model supports the same three-block load; they do not claim real load capacities. Both hollow-pillar designs and both strengthened paper-beam designs can work.

All fifteen activities require a prediction, two distinct comparison settings, a current successful test and an evidence-based explanation. Incorrect predictions remain valid starting ideas. Hints and retries do not remove lives. Fourteen activity definitions include a repeated waterproofing application in the finale. No real-world chemical preparation is required or instructed.
